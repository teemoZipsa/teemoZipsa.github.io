💡 Understanding the 9-point BCS Scale

1. How do I choose a BCS?

View your pet from above and from the side, then use both hands to feel the ribs. A visual check alone can be misleading under a long or dense coat.

  • 1–3, under ideal: ribs and bony points are easy to see or feel; the waist and abdominal tuck are pronounced.
  • 4–5, ideal: ribs are easy to feel without excess fat; a waist and abdominal tuck are visible.
  • 6–7, over ideal: added fat makes the ribs harder to feel and the waist less distinct.
  • 8–9, obese: ribs are difficult to feel under a heavy fat layer, with little or no waist.

Reference: AAHA 9-point BCS guidance, the WSAVA dog BCS chart, and the WSAVA cat BCS chart.

2. Does a breed weight range determine the result?

No. Healthy weight varies with sex, frame, age, and muscle mass even within one breed. The breed table is optional context for recording weight; it never changes the BCS group shown above.

3. Is BCS the same as human BMI?

No. Dogs and cats vary greatly in frame and body shape, so a single human-style weight-and-height formula is not appropriate. Veterinary teams use visual and hands-on BCS assessment for body fat and assess muscle separately with MCS.
